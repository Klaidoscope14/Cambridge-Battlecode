3. GAME RULES THAT MATTER FOR BOT DESIGN
======================================================================
- Main objective: tribute gems at altars. First team to tribute 1,000 gems wins immediately. At round 2,000, compare tributed gems, then lifetime gold earned, then champion kills; Team B wins if all tie.
- Killing all monsters in a camp group is not enough: a champion must occupy one of the group's spawn tiles uncontested for the capture timer. Capture usually takes five rounds; the new Capture emblem can reduce this to three when an eligible capturer owns it. Vacating/contesting can reset capture progress.
- Group rewards are shared among allied champions present when capture completes. More champions do not inherently speed capture.
- Camp respawn begins after the group reward is captured. Occupying a camp's spawn tile can freeze/prevent its respawn; an idle champion blocking spawn tiles can waste future farming value.
- Camps awaken when attacked, then attack the nearest champion in range. They sleep after two rounds without taking damage if nobody is in range. Camp damage and camp deaths can destroy the bags of champions; a final hit can be credited to a champion.
- Gems carried in a bag are vulnerable to death; tributed gems are permanent score. Tribute duration is based on the bag size when starting, but tribute completion includes the whole current bag, including gems acquired while channeling. Capture phase resolves before channels each round.
- Enemy damage does not by itself cancel tribute; altar locks and certain control effects can interrupt it. Altar locks can be cleared by three basic attacks, according to the research notes.
- Turn order: camp respawn / effects / capture / camp actions precede champion turns; teams alternate and starting side changes each round. Within a team, priority then draft order determines action order. Melee can move and attack on the same turn; ranged champions generally cannot move and make a basic attack in one turn unless an attack-reset mechanic restores attack allowance.
- Each turn normally includes movement, one basic attack, and one successful cast across ability slots subject to cooldowns/ability restrictions. Hidden enemies can block otherwise apparently legal movement destinations; retry/fallback logic matters.
- Maps vary in size and terrain. Walls block movement/line of sight, holes cannot be walked on (but some dashes can cross them), bushes affect vision, altar and spawn tiles have special occupancy restrictions.
- Cassi and Issac are coupled: when one dies, the other also dies. This affects pair risk and the amount of carried gems lost.
- Monoliths are large, high-reward but dangerous camps (20,000 HP; research summary cites 200 gems and 300 gold). V65 has an explicit monolith avoidance feature when an awake monolith is not the intended target.

4. REPLAY / MATCH DATA: WHAT TOP TEAMS DO
======================================================================
Data methodology recorded in Observations.md:
- The most recent 250 ranked matches for each of the top 10 teams: 2,500 collected.
- Game summaries were available for 2,469 top-10 games and 175 of the team's older games.
- Full replay sampling: 60 top-10 games (six per team, six maps per team) and 30 team games, recorded every three rounds through round 1,200.
- Map geometry and camp group data for the 70-map pool.
- The project defines early rounds as 0-299, middle 300-599, late 600+, and map categories by spawn-to-spawn walking distance: fight <=35, race >=70, otherwise normal.

Draft / champion performance:
- Top-10 pick rate: Marcella 84%, Vesper 82%, Vendetta 80%, Heart of Ruin 79%; Ryth 42%; Tavi 20%; Prism 18%; Cassi & Issac 11%; Willow 11%; Varg 9%; Ren 1%.
- Top-team core is a blend of farming and combat. Their draft is not just a fight lineup: Vesper, Marcella, and Vendetta are strong resource earners; Heart is useful in fights and still farms.
- Mean gold per champion in games where drafted: Vesper ~2,190; Marcella ~1,783; Vendetta ~1,221; Heart ~859. The precise table in Observations.md should be consulted for all per-champion columns.
- Top earners account for roughly 41-56% of team gold. Marcella earns additional gold from tributing gems (two gold per gem tributed).
- The team's earlier draft included Vesper, Tavi, Willow, and Varg; three of those four were among the least-picked champions in top-10 games. Willow was a particularly weak earner in the recorded sample.

Economy / clearing tempo:
- Top-team median first camp clear: round 19; first purchase: round 57; first tribute: round 77; first champion kill: round 173.
- Average team camp clears in rounds 0-299: top-10 14.8; older team bot 9.3. Middle rounds 300-599: top-10 14.9; team bot 9.6. The team's late clear count was higher, but the critical deficit was early/mid economy.
- Top-team gold at round 300 generally ranged around 1,023-1,465 across teams; team's prior bot was approximately 940. Intercontinental Fish was an outlier at ~1,465 average by round 300 and ~434 by round 100.
- Top teams clear many more cluster camps and small/wolf groups; their first clears are commonly clusters and wolves on their own half. The older team bot frequently prioritized big elk/golem groups instead.
- Top teams spend ~26% of early-game alive time farming (near a live camp) vs ~17% for the older bot. Team bots spend ~2.3-2.8% at spawn vs ~4.5-5.7% for top teams, implying more deliberate shopping/healing.
- A major research conclusion: farming tempo is a binding constraint. Strategy and items cannot rescue a bot that spends too many turns walking, waiting, choosing overly large groups, or dying to camps.

Banking / economy:
- Top-10 tribute sizes: early median ~21 gems (mean 27), mid median 25 (mean 34), late median 26 (mean 38). First tribute median round 77. Team's old bot generally banked larger bags, around median 30-33 in the side-by-side summary.
- Top teams make about 14-25 tributes per game. Average carried bag in the replay sample was 17, compared with 11.6 for the earlier team sample; Prism behaves as a special carrier and holds significantly larger bags.
- Marcella tributes ~5.4 times per game at ~41 gems each; because of two gold per tributed gem this is a substantial gold source.
- Banking policy should account for bag size, travel distance, threat, endgame closeout, whether the route is unsafe/blocked, and whether another tribute would secure victory. A single fixed bag threshold is a simplification.

Fights / survival:
- Top teams have enemy contact (enemy within five tiles) on ~18.8% of champion-round samples vs ~12.7% for the older team bot.
- Top teams are numerically ahead in ~57% of contacts and behind only around 10-13%. They don't simply fight more; they select favorable fights and avoid bad engagements.
- Top-team champions often step away at relatively high HP (median around 77%; individual champion medians around 68-74% in the replay table), then return. The earlier team bot retreated at around the low 70s by one replay measure, but its fight conversions/deaths were worse overall.
- Top-10 first kill occurs at median round 173, so they are not ordinarily all-in fighting right from the opening.
- Deaths correlate strongly with outcomes: top-10 wins average about 5.6 team deaths vs 12.1 in losses; losses have the reverse relation. Carrying gems into unwinnable fights can be catastrophic.
- Death locations are more common late in own half / mid in the recorded sample; away deaths were lower. When behind and making successful comebacks, teams continue collecting gems and tribute, take fights on their side, and push away from home when the fight state allows.

Vision / utility:
- Teams use ~77-231 wards per game; 47% own half, 47% middle, only 6% enemy half. About 60% of wards are within four tiles of a camp group and 23% within four tiles of an altar.
- Teleport crystals per game vary by team: top teams such as Trzy Kwaterki / NEETCoders use about five per game; some others use zero or one. This is map/team dependent, not a universal requirement.
- Eight of ten teams use altar locks, typically ~3.3-8.3 per team per game, to deny tributes.

Map / win pattern observations:
- Recorded map-mode win rates are roughly 52% fight maps, 54% normal, 53% race in the dataset; the categories are useful for behavior selection even though the top-team win rates are naturally influenced by the opponents in that sample.
- Particularly weak maps for the earlier team bot included the_pack, gold_rush, treasure_box, lanes, broken_curve, lost_world, and adgato_gemcombat1; the exact economy gaps are listed in Observations.md.
- A lead of 150+ gold at round 300 correlated with an 83% win rate in the sample; being behind by 150+ correlated with a 22% win rate. These are correlations, not guarantees.
- Better comebacks involved maintaining gem collection and tributes while lowering deaths; persistent deaths caused teams to stop farming safely and lose.

Core interpretation from Observations.md:
1. Draft for farming power, then fight with it; each body needs a useful economy role.
2. Farm cheap nearby clusters and wolves early, primarily on the own half; optimize for repeated clears rather than only the largest camp.
3. Spread out for farming, regroup for favorable fights. Do not make all champions follow a single target unless the camp is worth the squad.
4. Avoid bad fights and disengage early; return to heal/shop when needed.
5. Bank smaller bags more frequently than the earlier bot.
6. Use role-appropriate item choices and stack damage/durability based on champion.
7. Ward around camps and altars; use altar locks; buy/use teleport crystals conditionally.
8. Comebacks require fewer deaths, more tributes and selective expansion into enemy-half resources.

5. HISTORICAL RESEARCH / MEASURED V65 RESULTS
======================================================================
Research.md is the long-running record of measured tuning. Important lessons:

A. Validation discipline:
- Use a diagnostic panel to find weaknesses, not to accept a fix that was designed from those same games.
- Use a separate held-out panel B to accept/reject changes and ranked ladder results as the final judge.
- Compare a feature against the same code with the feature switched off; verify off == base first.
- A mirror win is not sufficient evidence. Some variants gained several mirror wins and lost more games against other opponent archetypes.
- Use focused tests for the mechanism, full map/opponent pools for acceptance, and SPRT / ladder comparison rather than choosing based on a handful of games.
- Local test bots are derivatives of the team's own code and do not faithfully reproduce ladder pick-offs, monolith strategies, stacking, spawn camping, etc. Local farming-rate metrics (gold at round 300 and clears by round 400) are still useful.
- Keep the fuel budget in view. A champion has about 100k fuel/turn (with special first/draft turn allowances); fuel exhaustion suspends/resumes the turn later and changes timing. Cache static information lazily, use exception guards around visibility-dependent SDK calls, and avoid redundant BFS rather than replacing BFS with slower algorithms.

B. High-level history:
- The known good submission named v65 in the supplied Research.md table is recorded as v61 + CI_DRAFT + MONO_AVOID. On the recorded full pool, v65 scored 1,471 vs 1,398 in the comparison (reported +184 wins / -111 losses), cleared 61.6 vs 55.4 camps, and finished games 32 rounds sooner. The same history reports deaths 7.5 vs 5.0, counting Cassi & Issac as two bodies. v65 was accepted after the panel: 11/20 in diagnostic panel A and 11/20 on held-out panel B.

- Draft: 3 bodies Vesper, Heart of Ruin, Vendetta; four adds Marcella; five adds Cassi & Issac. Fallbacks Ryth, Tavi.

7. CODE REVIEW: NOVA VS V65
======================================================================
This section collects the previous ChatGPT comparative code-review findings. Treat each as a source-based diagnosis/hypothesis to verify with targeted tests.

A. CAMP STATE / OBSERVATION LOGIC (Nova world.cpp; v65 bot.cpp::observe)
Nova observes visible camp occupancy and group state. When a group has visible tiles but no living camps detected, it sets a provisional respawn timer (round + 10) and queries group state. Its code handles reward-available and respawning variants but does not explicitly handle the camp-group-alive variant in that branch. If a living camp has wandered outside the observed spawn-tile/occupancy heuristic, Nova may falsely treat the group as absent. Once respawn_at > round, it skips further state() queries, potentially prolonging the wrong assumption. This can cause groups to be skipped or marked unavailable.
Recommended: handle all SDK state variants explicitly; alive must clear respawn_at and reward state. Never decide a full group is depleted solely from no living camp on the original visible spawn tile; use actual group state where safe and preserve uncertainty where vision is partial. Instrument transitions and test groups whose camps wander.
Caveat: do not assume this is the sole cause of Nova's low gold; confirm via traces/replays. v65 also uses safe_camps()/visibility-aware reads and has its own heuristics; compare exact behavior.

B. DEAD CHAMPION TURN HANDLING
Nova turn.cpp exits immediately on `if (!me.is_alive()) return;`. Therefore it does not shop while dead, doesn't clear local target/capture state, and doesn't clear its shared-memory claim. v65 has a dead-specific branch in play_turn(): optionally records death-avoid groups, calls shop(), resets local last_gems/target/capture state, and returns. However, in the supplied v65 config `DEAD_CLEARS_CLAIM=false`; the code for clearing a claim exists but is OFF due a past measured regression. Thus the certain v65 advantage is dead-time shopping and local reset; don't claim v65 actively clears shared claim under this exact config.
Recommended: let Nova buy items while dead if SDK permits it; clear/reset its own claim/state only after testing shared-memory lifecycle. Do not turn on shared dead-claim clearing blindly because Research.md says that experiment caused a regression.

C. ATTACK RESET / RANGED MOVE-THEN-ATTACK
Nova attack_group_camp() targets one camp, attacks it, then looks for a ready attack-reset ability; it checks if the originally selected pick is dead and breaks instead of refreshing and selecting another in-range camp. Enemy attack_best() also attempts a reset attack on the same selected target, rather than reselecting after a kill. This can waste a follow-up attack when the original target dies.
v65 attack_camp() attacks, refreshes the visible camp set with safe_camps(), reselects a best_in_range() target, and may attack again after an attack reset. v65 has restore_attack(), with RANGED_MOVE_RESET=true, to restore a ranged champion's attack allowance after movement when the mechanic is ready and applicable. Nova has no equivalent shared restore_attack() path.
Recommended: port the pattern, not necessarily exact helper signatures: after movement, use eligible reset to restore attack; after a kill, refresh/recompute targets before spending the reset; avoid invalid/stale handles. Test champions/ability types individually. Vendetta's cooldown-reset-on-kill passive and a specific attack-reset ability are not identical mechanics; ensure the implementation models each separately rather than assuming one loop covers everything.

D. DPS ESTIMATION / CAMP CLEARING
Nova estimates one scalar camp_dps from basic strength plus simple average contributions from abilities. It doesn't separately model single-target and multi-target damage or v65's splash passive and cooldown-reset-on-kill passive contribution. Nova group score consequently may misestimate how long multi-camp groups take to clear.
v65 estimates dps_single and dps_multi; counts attack resets, direct/AoE damage, splash-attack passive and reset-cooldowns-on-kill passive; then chooses single/multi DPS for a group. It also applies item/passive modifiers.
Recommended: adapt v65's single/multi damage approach to Nova, accounting for actual ability target restrictions, cooldown state and whether AoE hits multiple relevant camps. Improve estimated kill-time and helper requirements together. Ensure it remains conservative enough not to send a champion into a dangerous camp on an optimistic DPS estimate.

E. TARGET SCORING / MAP MODES / NEAR SWEEP
Nova uses value/time and static side weights (own 1.2 / mid 1.0 / away 0.6) and role bonuses. It doesn't implement a map-mode classifier in the supplied config/code described here. v65 has static group ownership and map-mode scoring: on fight maps it favors own half and devalues enemy half; on race maps it neutralizes the difference; normal maps moderately favor home. v65 also has NEAR_SWEEP=true: group within four tiles earns x2 value. Research notes indicate NEAR_SWEEP improved focused clear rates and became part of v65.
Recommended experiments: port map-mode scoring and near-sweep independently, keeping resource weighting and squad logic otherwise unchanged. Do not assume own-half bonuses are universally good; earlier OWN_HALF-only tests were not reliably accepted and race maps can lose tempo when a bot pairs too aggressively.

F. DISTANCE FIELDS / MOVEMENT / FUEL
Nova builds per-group distance fields lazily when there is enough fuel; otherwise path_to() falls back to a Chebyshev estimate plus a constant. Its farm() also uses a Chebyshev `near` value in some score/helper logic while rescore uses path_to distance. That makes candidate scores potentially incomparable on obstacle-heavy maps and risks mixing geometric with real path distances.
v65 caches static group fields and builds them incrementally from spare fuel at end of turn. The project's measured notes say BFS is an appropriate engine primitive; the main issue is redundant searches/fuel timing, not needing A* or Dijkstra. Some optimizations reduced fuel suspensions but did not improve strength and were left off.
Recommended: use one consistent distance estimate for score and helper/wait logic; refine promising candidates with cached path fields. Profile fuel. Avoid constructing all fields at once or adding path computations that create suspension timing changes. Preserve exact behavior tests where possible.

G. SQUAD / CLAIM / WAIT LOGIC
Nova's needed_helpers() depends on this champion's HP, DPS and current health allowance. `score()` compares claimers against that local requirement; different champions may therefore disagree about whether the group is adequately staffed. Nova's configured HELP_WAIT=10 and farm wait logic effectively adds distance to the timeout, conflicting with NewStrategy.md's explicit “never wait more than four rounds for help.”
v65 uses helpers_for()/required_helpers(), a pooled option for multi-camp groups, `allies_near()`, claim counting, a configurable missing-helper time penalty, squad claim bonus, and shorter regroup / give-up clocks (`LESS_WAIT=true`, regroup 4, give up 12, penalty 10 per missing helper). It has more complex options for rich groups / very large groups, not all applied universally.
Recommended: separate a consistent squad-size requirement based on camp/team mechanics from an individual champ's survival judgement. Reduce idle wait only after checking helper convergence; don't remove all squads because some large groups need them. Measure rounds spent waiting and early clears.

H. CAPTURE COORDINATION / SHARED MEMORY
Nova writes target claims in bytes 0-5 but does not implement v65's capture notification mechanism. v65 uses one shared-memory byte to broadcast captured group ID with a parity bit so allies refresh local respawn knowledge. v65's turn flow also tracks last_gems/last_gold and capture_group to decide whether its capture likely completed.
Recommended: consider porting the capture broadcast pattern if stale group state causes duplicate travel or idle actions. Keep the eight-byte memory layout coordinated across all champion processes; don't reuse a byte without documenting collisions.

I. FIGHTING, THREAT, OUTMATCHED, JOIN
Nova assess() computes a Lanchester-like ratio from champions around the current bot (radius six) and includes visible/remembered enemies. Its join() then checks that same current-position ratio before joining a fight near an ally. An ally can be some distance away, so the ratio can reflect the bot's neighborhood rather than the prospective fight. This is a logical flaw candidate.
v65 builds a more detailed threat picture: visible foes with ability cooldown metadata (stun/dash/reset/burst), ghosts with movement slack, burst_here, danger tiles, outmatched state, exchange guard, and a fitted fight-outcome model (FIT_FIGHT=true). It also has dedicated routines for channel interruption, help calls, answering calls, convergence, ranged safety, and enemy focusing. Some optional fight simulator flags are off; inspect the active branch and not just the functions' existence.
Recommended: evaluate ally-join power at the ally/fight location, include what can actually reach that location, and distinguish visible/remembered threat from irrelevant ghosts. Use incoming burst / lethal danger in retreat decisions. Avoid blindly raising a global retreat threshold: Research.md shows some retreat and burst heuristics made results worse or caused champions to be run down. More sophisticated combat code should be introduced incrementally and measured.

J. ENEMY TARGETING / ABILITIES
Nova generally attacks killable enemies, then low-health shares, and selects some spells by smallest HP or highest DPS. v65 has a richer target priority function using killability against its burst, gems carried, channel state, and optionally the coupled Cassi/Issac pair. v65's ability routing includes dash strikes with landing effects, camp crowd control, support healing/shields, and channel-breaking logic.
Recommended: port target-priority features only when relevant and available in the actual current SDK. A tribute interrupt can be much more valuable than a routine attack because ordinary damage does not cancel tribute. A kill that saves a large bag may have unusually high value.

K. BANKING / ECONOMY PRIORITY
Nova's turn order is observe -> fight (which often returns immediately) -> deny tribute -> bank -> home/heal -> join -> farm -> ward. Thus, if Nova's fight() reports an action, it skips the later deny/bank logic that turn. This can be wrong when an urgent altar lock could interrupt a tribute or when a high-risk gem carrier should bank/escape instead of routine combat.
v65 applies retreat assessment early, then evaluates urgent threat, altar locks/channel disruption, fights, and later banking with multiple guardrails. Its bank logic includes closeout, carrying risk, recent PvP, lead/behind state, threatened/remembered altars, detour banking, reachable altars, altar locks and a fallback when banking is blocked. But do not assume every optional guard is enabled. Current v65 has base BANK_BAG=48; the additional CARRY_RISK flag is true and uses CR_BAG=20 after recent enemy sighting, and PVP banking / other thresholds are configuration dependent.
Recommended: use urgency-aware action arbitration rather than an unconditional priority list. Emergency retreat and tribute denial should pre-empt routine attacks; winning/decisive fights may still take priority over low-risk farming. Keep banking behavior tied to bag value, threat and route.

L. MONOLITH AVOIDANCE
Nova excludes monoliths as farm targets, but the supplied movement/fight logic does not implement the full proposed rule of avoiding tiles in an awake monolith's attack reach + one. A champion may therefore avoid directly targeting a monolith and still walk into its danger area.
v65 has MONO_AVOID=true; Research.md reports a substantial monolith problem in the older bot (194 monolith-map games: top opponents cleared about 0.64 monoliths/game vs 0.02 for our team, and 28% of our deaths were within five tiles of a monolith). MONO_TEAM / dominant-monolith whole-team approach was tested separately and failed.
Recommended: model awake monolith danger explicitly in path/target movement, without assuming the right move is always to fight the monolith.

M. SHOPPING / ITEMS / DRAFT
Nova shops when on spawn, has plans favoring Treasure then Jungle then Bruiser, and may return home at >=300 gold when close or when hurt. v65's current next_item() path is governed by POWER_OPENER, PROSPECTOR_MAPS, Jungle opener and PO_BRUISER; don't infer the actual buy order only from historical comment names. v65's draft first attempts Cassi & Issac when available and then, with RANGED_META, opens Vesper, Tavi, and a third option (default Willow unless MELEE_OPENER overrides it). This is different from Nova's static top-10-core order Vesper / Heart / Vendetta / Marcella / C&I.
Most important strategic evidence: multiple tests of the top-10 melee draft, including Heart low-HP and Marcella capture-role tuning, were much worse than v65. Do not replace v65's draft / build blindly based on pick frequency. First make Nova's behavior work, then test draft and item policy as an isolated variable.

N. WARDS / TELEPORT / DASH
Nova places wards every eight rounds and chooses a tile near its target/altar and closer to enemy spawn, but its supplied code has no teleport-crystal shortcut and does not use targeted dash strike as v65 does. v65 has WARD_SCOUT=true every 10 rounds under its preconditions, TP_SHORTCUT=true on sufficiently wall-heavy maps when predicted travel saved is large, and DASH_STRIKE=true.
Recommended: these may help on specific maps but are secondary to core economy/combat defects. Implement conditional use (safety and net-turn benefit) rather than burning mobility cooldowns by default.

O. CAPTURE TURN ACTIONS
Nova's capture branch attacks a target in range but may not use other useful safe casts while waiting on a reward. v65 has broader support/camp-casting routines and designated capture handling. Explore using valid safe casts while holding a capture tile, but do not move off or otherwise reset the capture.

9. TESTING PLAN / METRICS

Preserve an immutable baseline of Nova-v0 and the current v65 source/build. Implement each change behind a feature flag or as a single isolated change, with a control build using the same source and the feature disabled.

Suggested progression:
1. Build both baselines cleanly. Verify SDK / engine version and cache are correct (research warns the engine 2.0 backpack/API change can silently make stale builds fail to act or behave incorrectly; clean stale build caches when needed).
2. Run diagnostic micro-tests focused on the exact mechanism: camp-state transitions, ranged move/reset/attack, kill chaining, dead shopping, helper wait, captured group updates, and monolith path avoidance.
3. For each farming change, compare the same maps and sides against the control and v65. Track round of first clear, clears by round 100/200/300/400, gold at rounds 100/200/300, time spent on camp / walking / waiting / at spawn.
4. For each combat change, track kills, deaths, traded deaths, gems lost per death, carry bag before death, contact situations, fight outcomes and whether the intervention helped.
5. Compare on the same four Nova-v0 maps (00eight, 02fields, 01shortcut, 00lanes, both sides) for continuity but do not treat only these maps as acceptance.
6. Use the existing diagnostic panel and separate held-out panel B. The Research.md protocol says not to accept a change on the same replay-derived panel used to design it. Run a broad pool / SPRT or ladder comparison for acceptance.
7. Check opponent-level results. A global gain can hide regression against one archetype (ranged poke, melee dive, gem-lane stacker, lock user, monolith taker, spawn camper). Research explicitly advises not accepting a feature just because mirror results improved.
8. Compare action/log outputs and fuel. Avoid optimizations that change decisions merely due to turn suspension/timing without a clear strength improvement. Test feature-off == baseline and bit-identical games where neither build suspends when refactoring behavior intended to be equivalent.

Minimum scorecard to collect for every build:
- win/loss, rounds to finish, tributed gems;
- gold by round 100, 200, 300;
- camp clears in early/mid phases and by group kind (cluster/small/big/monolith), plus own/mid/away location;
- number of tributes, first tribute round, mean/median tribute size;
- deaths, traded deaths, kill/assist, gems lost per death;
- round/time share spent farming, travelling, waiting, shopping/healing and fighting;
- stuck-target count, target switches, dead/respawn state corrections, capture completion latency, failed movement and failed ability calls;
- fuel minimum / suspensions per game and turn stage at failure.

Do not tune several coupled variables in one experiment (for example draft + item build + fight threshold + map score). If multiple features must be combined, test them individually first, then test the combination to detect interactions.
